# Aerospace & Defense Calibration Business Impact & ROI

## Cost of Non-Compliance

### NADCAP Audit Failures
A NADCAP audit failure results in loss of accreditation, which typically means loss of contracts with major OEMs (Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Raytheon). Re-audit costs $15,000-$30,000, but the real impact is lost revenue during the 3-6 month remediation period. Suppliers report revenue losses of $500,000 to $2 million during NADCAP suspension.

### AS9100 Nonconformances
Major nonconformances related to Section 7.1.5 (Monitoring and Measuring Resources) require corrective action within 60 days. Calibration-related NCs are among the top 5 most common AS9100 audit findings. Each major NC costs $20,000-$75,000 in root cause analysis, corrective action implementation, and effectiveness verification.

### Flight Hardware Scrap
Out-of-tolerance measurement equipment can produce flight hardware that fails final inspection. Scrapped aerospace parts cost $10,000 to $500,000 per part depending on material (titanium, Inconel) and machining complexity. A single out-of-tolerance CMM can generate $100,000+ in scrap before detection.

### First Article Inspection (FAI) Failures
FAI failures attributable to measurement uncertainty require complete re-inspection and often re-manufacturing. Each FAI cycle costs $5,000-$50,000 in labor and machine time. Repeated FAI failures can result in removal from an OEM's approved supplier list.

### Customer Escapes and Recalls
If nonconforming parts reach the customer, containment costs average $50,000-$250,000. Safety-critical escapes on flight hardware can result in airworthiness directives, grounding of aircraft, and liability exposure in the tens of millions.

## ROI of Proactive Calibration

### Scrap and Rework Reduction
Aerospace manufacturers with optimized calibration intervals (using statistical methods per ANSI/NCSL Z540.3) report 20-35% reduction in measurement-related scrap. For a shop producing $5 million in annual product, this represents $200,000-$500,000 in saved material and machine time.

### Audit Confidence
Maintaining a 17025-accredited calibration program reduces NADCAP and AS9100 audit findings by 50-70%. This translates to fewer corrective actions, shorter audit cycles, and sustained customer confidence.

### Measurement Uncertainty Reduction
Reducing measurement uncertainty through proper calibration and guard-banding allows tighter process control, enabling acceptance of parts that would otherwise be borderline rejects. This can recover 5-10% of parts previously scrapped as marginal.

## Decision-Maker Pain Points
- Quality managers must demonstrate measurement traceability to NIST for every critical dimension
- Program managers face schedule delays when calibration lapses halt production
- Contracts require AS9100 and often NADCAP accreditation — losing either means losing the contract
- Cost estimators undervalue calibration program investment, leading to reactive spending at 5-10x the preventive cost
