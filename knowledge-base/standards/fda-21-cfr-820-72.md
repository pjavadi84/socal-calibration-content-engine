# FDA 21 CFR Part 820.72 — Inspection, Measuring, and Test Equipment

## Overview

21 CFR Part 820 is the FDA's Quality System Regulation (QSR) for medical device manufacturers. Section 820.72 specifically addresses calibration requirements for equipment used in the production and quality control of medical devices.

## Exact Requirements (820.72)

### Section 820.72(a) — Control of Inspection, Measuring, and Test Equipment

Each manufacturer shall ensure that all inspection, measuring, and test equipment, including mechanical, automated, or electronic inspection and test equipment, is suitable for its intended purposes and is capable of producing valid results. Each manufacturer shall establish and maintain procedures to ensure that equipment is routinely calibrated, inspected, maintained, and adjusted.

Specific requirements:
- Equipment must be calibrated against **traceable standards** (NIST or equivalent national standards)
- Calibration must be performed at **defined intervals** based on the type of equipment, the extent and frequency of use, and other conditions affecting the measurement
- The **limits for accuracy and precision** must be documented
- Provisions must be made for **remedial action** when accuracy and precision limits are not met
- Calibration **records must be maintained**

### Section 820.72(b) — Calibration Standards

Where calibration standards do not exist, the manufacturer must document the basis used for calibration.

## What This Means in Practice

### Documentation Requirements

Medical device manufacturers must maintain:
1. A **calibration schedule** listing all controlled equipment
2. **Calibration procedures** for each equipment type
3. **Acceptance criteria** (tolerance limits) for each measurement parameter
4. **Calibration records** showing as-found and as-left data
5. **Out-of-tolerance investigation records** — when equipment is found out of tolerance, the manufacturer must assess the impact on product that was measured since the last good calibration

### Impact Assessment (Critical FDA Requirement)

When a calibrated instrument is found **out of tolerance**, FDA requires:
- Investigation of all product tested/measured with that instrument since its last known good calibration
- Risk assessment for affected product batches
- Potential product recall or additional testing if patient safety could be compromised
- Documentation of the investigation and conclusions (this is frequently cited in FDA 483 observations)

### Common FDA 483 Observations Related to Calibration

FDA 483 is the form used to document observations of non-compliance during FDA inspections. Common calibration-related findings:

1. **No calibration program** — equipment used without any calibration schedule
2. **Overdue calibrations** — instruments used past their calibration due date
3. **No traceability** — calibrations not traceable to NIST or national standards
4. **Inadequate procedures** — calibration procedures missing acceptance criteria
5. **No impact assessment** — out-of-tolerance findings not investigated for product impact
6. **Incomplete records** — missing as-found data, environmental conditions, or uncertainty

## Equipment Commonly Calibrated Under 820.72

| Equipment | Typical Parameters | Typical Tolerance |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Analytical balances | Mass measurement | ±0.1 mg to ±1 mg depending on class |
| Dimensional gauges (calipers, micrometers) | Length measurement | ±0.01 mm to ±0.05 mm |
| Temperature equipment (autoclaves, ovens) | Temperature | ±1°C to ±5°C depending on process |
| Pressure gauges | Pressure | ±0.5% to ±2% of full scale |
| Electrical test equipment | Voltage, current, resistance | Per manufacturer specification |
| Force/torque testers | Force/torque | ±1% to ±5% of reading |
| pH meters | pH | ±0.01 to ±0.1 pH units |
| Flow meters | Volume/mass flow rate | ±1% to ±5% of reading |

## Connection to ISO 13485

ISO 13485:2016 (Medical devices — Quality management systems) Section 7.6 mirrors FDA 820.72 requirements. Companies certified to ISO 13485 and FDA 820 must satisfy both:
- ISO 13485 Section 7.6 requires documented procedures for monitoring and measurement
- Measurement uncertainty must be determined when ensuring conformity to requirements
- Records must be maintained per ISO 13485 Section 4.2.5

## Southern California Medical Device Industry Context

Southern California has a significant medical device manufacturing presence:
- **Orange County**: Major hub for medical device companies (Edwards Lifesciences in Irvine, Masimo in Irvine, ICU Medical in San Clemente)
- **Los Angeles**: Hologic in Marlborough has operations; numerous smaller manufacturers
- **San Diego**: ResMed, NuVasive, DexCom, Tandem Diabetes Care
- The FDA's Los Angeles District Office oversees compliance for Southern California medical device manufacturers

All of these companies — and their suppliers — require regular calibration services that comply with 21 CFR 820.72.
