# ISO/IEC 17025:2017 — General Requirements for Testing and Calibration Laboratories

## Overview

ISO/IEC 17025:2017 is the international standard for the competence of testing and calibration laboratories. It was last revised in 2017, replacing the 2005 edition. Accreditation to this standard is the primary benchmark for calibration laboratory quality worldwide.

## Key Clauses Relevant to Calibration Services

### Clause 6.2 — Personnel

- Laboratories must ensure all personnel performing calibration activities are competent based on appropriate education, training, experience, and demonstrated skills.
- Competence requirements must be documented for each function influencing calibration results.
- Personnel must be supervised until deemed competent and must be re-evaluated periodically.

### Clause 6.4 — Equipment

- All equipment used for calibration that can influence measurement results must itself be calibrated.
- Equipment must be calibrated before being placed or returned to service when calibration status indicates it is due.
- Calibration must establish traceability to SI units through an unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty.
- Reference standards must be calibrated by a body that can provide metrological traceability to SI.

### Clause 6.5 — Metrological Traceability

- Calibration laboratories must establish and maintain metrological traceability of their measurement results to the International System of Units (SI).
- This is achieved through an unbroken chain of calibrations, each contributing to measurement uncertainty.
- **NIST traceability** (in the US context) means the calibration chain ultimately traces back to standards maintained by the National Institute of Standards and Technology.
- Each step in the traceability chain must have a documented measurement uncertainty.

### Clause 7.1 — Review of Requests, Tenders, and Contracts

- The laboratory must have a procedure for reviewing calibration requests to ensure it has the capability and resources to meet the customer's requirements.
- The method of calibration must be agreed upon with the customer.

### Clause 7.6 — Evaluation of Measurement Uncertainty

- Calibration laboratories **must evaluate measurement uncertainty** for all calibrations.
- The evaluation must take into account all significant contributions to uncertainty.
- Measurement uncertainty must be reported on calibration certificates.
- Methods for evaluating uncertainty must follow the Guide to the Expression of Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM) or equivalent.

### Clause 7.8 — Reporting of Results (Calibration Certificates)

- Calibration certificates must include:
  - The calibration results with measurement uncertainty
  - The conditions under which calibrations were made (temperature, humidity)
  - A statement of traceability to SI
  - The date of calibration
  - Identity of the items calibrated
  - The calibration method used
- Results must be reported accurately, clearly, unambiguously, and objectively.

### Clause 8.7 — Management System Options

- Laboratories can implement a management system conforming to the requirements of ISO/IEC 17025 (Option A) or operate within an ISO 9001 management system (Option B).
- Most calibration labs use Option A, which is self-contained within 17025.

## Practical Implications for Customers

1. **Always verify accreditation scope** — a lab can be ISO 17025 accredited for some calibrations but not others. The accreditation certificate lists specific measurement parameters and ranges.
2. **"ISO 17025 compliant" is not the same as "ISO 17025 accredited"** — any lab can claim compliance; only accredited labs have been independently audited.
3. **Measurement uncertainty matters** — a calibration certificate without measurement uncertainty has limited value; it doesn't tell you whether the instrument is actually within its specifications.
4. **Calibration vs. adjustment** — ISO 17025 calibration determines the relationship between instrument readings and known values. It does not necessarily include adjustment. These are separate services.

## Common Calibration Intervals (Based on ISO 17025 and Industry Practice)

There is no single required interval — ISO 17025 requires labs to establish intervals based on evidence:

| Equipment Type | Typical Interval | Factors Affecting Interval |
|---------------|-----------------|---------------------------|
| Precision scales/balances | 6–12 months | Usage frequency, environment, tolerance requirements |
| Pressure gauges | 12 months | Process criticality, vibration exposure |
| Torque wrenches | 6–12 months | Usage frequency, drop incidents |
| Multimeters/DMMs | 12 months | Stability of instrument, measurement criticality |
| Thermometers (digital) | 12 months | Sensor type, environment |
| Calipers/micrometers | 12 months | Handling, storage conditions |

These intervals should be **adjusted based on calibration history** — if an instrument consistently returns within tolerance, intervals can be extended; if it shows drift, intervals should be shortened (per ILAC-G24 methodology).
