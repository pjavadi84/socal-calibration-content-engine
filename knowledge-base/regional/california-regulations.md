# California Regulatory Requirements for Calibration and Measurement

## CDFA — Division of Measurement Standards

The California Department of Food and Agriculture (CDFA), Division of Measurement Standards (DMS), is the state authority responsible for ensuring accuracy in all commercial weighing and measuring devices. DMS establishes statewide standards and provides oversight for the 58 county Departments of Weights and Measures (also known as county sealers).

### Organizational Structure

- **State level**: CDFA DMS sets policy, adopts regulations (California Code of Regulations Title 4, Division 9), and maintains the state's primary measurement standards traceable to NIST.
- **County level**: 58 county sealers enforce weights and measures laws locally. They inspect and test commercial weighing and measuring devices, conduct package checking (net content verification), and investigate consumer complaints.
- **Type Evaluation**: DMS operates the California Type Evaluation Program (CTEP), which evaluates and approves weighing and measuring devices before they can be used commercially in the state. NTEP (National Type Evaluation Program) Certificates of Conformance are generally accepted.

### Key Regulatory Requirements

California Business and Professions Code Division 5, commencing with Section 12001, establishes:

- **Section 12107**: All weighing and measuring devices used for commercial purposes must be sealed (approved) by the county sealer before use and at regular intervals thereafter.
- **Section 12210**: It is unlawful to use any weighing or measuring device for commercial purposes that has been condemned, or to remove any tag or seal placed on a device by a sealer.
- **Section 12500-12510 (Net Container Content)**: Packaged commodities must contain at least the declared net quantity. Average net quantity must equal or exceed the labeled quantity. County sealers conduct inspections per NIST Handbook 133 methodology.

### Commercial Weighing Device Registration

All commercial weighing and measuring devices must be registered with the county sealer. Registration requirements include:

- Application filed with the county Department of Weights and Measures.
- Annual registration fees (varying by county; typically $20-$200 per device depending on type and capacity).
- Initial inspection and test before commercial use.
- Periodic inspections by county sealers (annually for most devices; more frequently for high-use devices).
- Devices found out of tolerance are tagged with a "Condemned" notice and must be repaired and re-tested before resuming commercial use.

## California Air Resources Board (CARB)

CARB regulates air quality in California under the California Health and Safety Code and Title 17 of the California Code of Regulations. Calibration requirements apply to:

### Continuous Emissions Monitoring Systems (CEMS)

- **Title 17, Section 95100-95163 (Mandatory Greenhouse Gas Reporting Regulation)**: Facilities reporting GHG emissions must operate and maintain CEMS calibrated per 40 CFR Part 60 or Part 75 methodologies.
- **Daily calibration drift tests**: Zero and upscale calibration checks required every 24 hours of operation. Allowable drift: +/-2.5% of span for most parameters.
- **Quarterly cylinder gas audits (CGA)**: Using EPA Protocol gases or NIST-traceable reference gases.
- **Relative Accuracy Test Audit (RATA)**: Annual testing comparing CEMS to reference method measurements. RATA must demonstrate relative accuracy within 10% (20% for SO2 at low concentrations).

### Source Testing Equipment

- Stack testing equipment (Method 1-30 per 40 CFR Part 60, Appendix A) must use calibrated instruments: pitot tubes (per EPA Method 2), temperature sensors, gas analyzers (Method 6C, 7E, 10), and particulate sampling trains.
- Portable emissions measurement systems (PEMS) for vehicle testing must meet CARB's own certification requirements.

### Ambient Air Monitoring

- CARB and local Air Quality Management Districts (AQMD) operate ambient air monitoring networks. Instruments (PM2.5 samplers, ozone analyzers, NO2 analyzers) are calibrated per 40 CFR Part 58 quality assurance requirements.

## Cal/OSHA — Division of Occupational Safety and Health

California operates its own OSHA-approved State Plan through the Division of Occupational Safety and Health (DOSH), commonly known as Cal/OSHA. Cal/OSHA adopts and enforces its own standards under Title 8 of the California Code of Regulations, which in most cases meet or exceed federal OSHA requirements.

### Key Calibration-Related Standards

- **Section 5155 (Airborne Contaminants)**: Permissible Exposure Limits (PELs) that are often more stringent than federal OSHA. Monitoring requires calibrated personal air sampling pumps (flow rate verification before and after each sample) and calibrated direct-reading instruments.
- **Section 5189 (Process Safety Management)**: Mirrors federal 29 CFR 1910.119 but Cal/OSHA enforces it more aggressively. Requires calibrated process instrumentation (pressure relief devices, level indicators, temperature sensors).
- **Section 5194 (Hazard Communication)**: While not directly a calibration requirement, analytical methods used to determine chemical hazards require calibrated instruments.
- **Confined Space Entry (Section 5157-5158)**: Atmospheric testing equipment (multi-gas monitors for O2, LEL, CO, H2S) must be calibrated per manufacturer specifications, with bump testing before each use. Cal/OSHA requires documentation of calibration and bump test records.

Cal/OSHA penalties for violations are generally higher than federal OSHA. Serious violations can carry penalties up to $25,000 per violation (versus $16,131 federal maximum), making calibration compliance documentation particularly important.

## Proposition 65 Implications

The Safe Drinking Water and Toxic Enforcement Act of 1986 (Proposition 65) requires businesses to warn Californians about significant exposures to chemicals that cause cancer, birth defects, or reproductive harm.

- **Analytical testing**: Laboratories performing Proposition 65 compliance testing (determining chemical concentrations in products) must use calibrated analytical instruments (ICP-MS, GC-MS, HPLC) with NIST-traceable reference standards.
- **Measurement accuracy**: Given that Proposition 65 safe harbor levels are extremely low (e.g., lead at 0.5 micrograms/day), analytical methods require calibrated instruments with detection limits well below regulatory thresholds.
- **Environmental monitoring**: Facilities must calibrate emissions monitoring equipment to demonstrate compliance with discharge limits that may trigger Proposition 65 warnings.

## Cannabis Industry Measurement Requirements

The California Department of Cannabis Control (DCC), which consolidated the former Bureau of Cannabis Control, Department of Food and Agriculture cannabis programs, and CDPH manufacturing programs, regulates measurement in the cannabis industry:

- **California Code of Regulations, Title 4, Division 19**: Commercial cannabis weighing and measuring must use NTEP-approved, county-sealed scales. All cannabis products must be weighed at multiple points in the supply chain (cultivation, manufacturing, distribution, retail).
- **Accuracy requirements**: Scales used for retail cannabis sales must meet NIST Handbook 44 Class II specifications. Manufacturing and distribution scales must meet Class III minimum.
- **Track and trace (METRC)**: All weight measurements entered into the state's track-and-trace system must be from calibrated, sealed devices. Discrepancies trigger compliance investigations.
- **Testing laboratories**: Cannabis testing laboratories must maintain ISO 17025 accreditation (required by BPC Section 26100), which mandates calibrated analytical instruments (HPLC for potency, GC-MS/ICP-MS for pesticides and heavy metals, moisture analyzers).

## DTSC — Department of Toxic Substances Control

The Department of Toxic Substances Control (DTSC) regulates hazardous waste management under the California Health and Safety Code, Chapter 6.5 (commencing with Section 25100), and Title 22 of the California Code of Regulations:

### Environmental Monitoring Calibration

- **Groundwater monitoring**: Wells at hazardous waste treatment, storage, and disposal facilities (TSDFs) require calibrated sampling equipment — flow cells, multi-parameter sondes (pH, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, ORP, turbidity), and water level indicators.
- **Air monitoring at remediation sites**: Photoionization detectors (PIDs), flame ionization detectors (FIDs), and particulate monitors used during soil remediation must be calibrated daily with certified reference gases.
- **Laboratory analysis**: Certified environmental laboratories performing analyses for DTSC programs must maintain calibration per EPA SW-846 methods (e.g., Method 6010D for ICP-OES, Method 8260D for volatile organics by GC-MS). Initial calibration verification (ICV), continuing calibration verification (CCV), and calibration blank requirements are strictly enforced.

### Corrective Action Sites

California has over 1,200 active DTSC-overseen cleanup sites. Each generates ongoing calibration demand for field monitoring instruments, sampling equipment, and laboratory analytical instruments used to verify cleanup progress against site-specific remediation goals.

## County Sealers of Weights and Measures — Key SoCal Offices

- **Los Angeles County**: Department of Agricultural Commissioner / Weights and Measures (largest in the state)
- **Orange County**: OC Weights and Measures (Agricultural Commissioner's Office)
- **San Diego County**: Department of Agriculture, Weights and Measures
- **San Bernardino County**: Department of Agriculture / Weights and Measures
- **Riverside County**: Agricultural Commissioner's Office
- **Ventura County**: Agricultural Commissioner's Office / Weights and Measures

Each county office enforces device registration, periodic inspection, and package checking within its jurisdiction. Businesses operating across multiple counties must register devices in each county.
